VisConf: Quality-Aware Consensus for Best-of-N in Vision-Language Model Reasoning

Muhammad Usman, Muhammad Qasim Ayub, Usman Ahad, Muhammad Tahir

NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Grounded and Faithful Vision-Language Models for Real-World Deployment (VLM4RWD), Sydney, 2026

Best-of-N sampling improves vision-language reasoning, but majority voting treats every sampled answer as equally trustworthy, even when a rollout ignores the image or the model is uncertain. We propose a quality-aware alternative that requires no retraining.

  • VisConf score. Scores each reasoning rollout by combining visual groundedness, measured from the model’s attention to image tokens, with confidence measured by DKL(U‖p) over the output distribution.
  • Rank-Weighted Consensus (RWC). Combines VisConf scores with answer agreement, so that the final answer across up to 32 rollouts is chosen by quality-aware voting rather than frequency alone.
  • Results. Verifier-free and training-free. Across three VLMs on MathVista, MMMU-Pro and MMStar with 8, 16 and 32 rollouts, VisConf reached 56.36% mean accuracy and outperformed all evaluated baselines in 24 of 27 settings, with the largest gains when correct reasoning paths are outnumbered.

In progressComputational biology

Mechanistic Interpretability for Post-Translational Modification Prediction

Motivation

Post-translational modifications (PTMs), such as phosphorylation, switch protein function on and off, and their misregulation is linked to disease. Because mapping PTM sites experimentally is slow and expensive, computational predictors are widely used. Recent methods such as SAPP and GraphPhos perform well, but why they work remains unclear, which limits how they can be improved.

Approach

  • Faithful baselines. Reproduce SAPP and GraphPhos under a shared evaluation protocol so that later gains can be attributed to specific design choices.
  • Sequence and structure together. A general pipeline that predicts PTMs from raw protein sequence, using protein language model embeddings, and from residue-level structural context.
  • Beyond solvent accessibility. Replace summaries based only on relative solvent accessibility (RSA) with contact graphs, structure-availability masks and confidence-aware geometry, so that uncertain predicted structures are not treated as ground truth.
  • Mechanistic analysis. Probe trained models to identify which sequence motifs and structural signals drive their predictions, and use these signals to design more accurate and robust architectures.

Open questions

Which signals drive accurate PTM-site prediction: local sequence motifs, solvent exposure, or a residue’s 3D neighborhood? Do these mechanisms hold across modification types?

In progressOpen-world learning

Open-Set Recognition in Federated Learning

Motivation

Deployed classifiers inevitably encounter inputs from classes unseen during training. Federated learning compounds the problem: each client observes a skewed subset of classes, data cannot be pooled, and no single client has a clear picture of what “unknown” looks like. Models must therefore reject unknown inputs while remaining accurate on known classes.

Approach

  • Synthetic negatives in latent space. Augmentations of known-class features generate synthetic negative examples, providing a training signal for “unknown” without real outlier data.
  • Equal-affinity regularizer. A KL-divergence term encourages each synthetic negative to have equal affinity to every learned class prototype, placing unknowns away from all known classes rather than near any one of them.
  • Discriminative-subspace hypothesis. A proposed explanation for why some latent augmentations improve open-set recognition while others do not: they help when they act along the directions that separate classes.

Open questions

Which representation directions matter most for separating known from unknown inputs, and how does client heterogeneity change them? Answering these questions should allow augmentations to be designed in a principled way rather than found by trial and error.

Enviromon: Game-Based Environmental Learning for Children in Pakistan

Motivation

Smog, river pollution and habitat loss directly affect children in Pakistan, yet most learning material on these topics is text-heavy and available only in English. We asked how familiar game mechanics could make environmental issues engaging and accessible to young children.

Design

  • Mechanics that mirror the problem. An explorable pixel-art world with nine missions, each mapping an environmental issue to a classic arcade mechanic: clearing smog clouds, sorting landfill waste, routing pipes to seal a factory leak, and a Flappy Bird-style bee flight through pesticide clouds.
  • Learning before play. Each mission begins by explaining the issue, why it is harmful and how to play. Wildlife rescue missions, featuring species such as the snow leopard and the dolphin, combine short quizzes with animals children recognize.
  • Accessible by design. Fully bilingual in English and Urdu, playable on mobile devices with an on-screen joystick, and supported by coins, XP and badges to sustain motivation.

Project and research · Health technology

Mederium: patient experience and clinical data quality in Pakistan

Mederium is a subsidized health-technology platform for clinics in Pakistan, designed for patients and doctors who may have limited experience with digital tools. It also serves as a research setting for studying what improves patient satisfaction and how structured clinical data can be collected during routine visits.

mederium.comLive
mederium.com

Research questions

  • Do direct booking, accurate answers to patient questions and automated follow-ups improve satisfaction and continuity of care?
  • Can voice-first documentation make clinical records more complete without adding to a doctor’s workload?

System

  • Structured records by voice. Doctors dictate in Urdu, English or both, and the system drafts an ICD-10-coded record for the doctor to review.
  • Grounded assistance. A patient assistant answers from each clinic’s own knowledge base, on the web and on WhatsApp, while a doctor copilot acts only after explicit confirmation.
